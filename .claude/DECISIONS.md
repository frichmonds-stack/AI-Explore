- **React Router v7 client-side routing** — single-page app feel with no server required. Fits static hosting.

## Strategy (2026-07-29, session 7 — the stress test: Form survives; the score aggregates by mean; the tool card carries two ratings)

Seventh session of the rubric arc, and the first **adversarial** one — the owner's instruction was to attack what session 6 had just ruled rather than extend it. Six findings were put up against `Polish` and `Format`; the owner agreed with all six, and the one with no fix attached (**within-task weighting**) drove the rest of the session into aggregation, which produced the card architecture.

**Net: the outputs score aggregates by mean, in two levels, into five owner-authored bands. The tool card carries a *measured* outputs number and a *judged* recommendation badge, which are different objects with different warrants.** Substance is still untouched.

**The six Form findings (agent-raised, all owner-agreed — *"I agree with everything except I don't know a solution to 6"*).** The findings are endorsed; the wording of the fixes is agent-drafted and was not line-edited. In [`delivery-rubric.md`](delivery-rubric.md):
  1. **The Brief↔Format seam was missing.** The Purpose↔Format seam was drawn on 07-29; the Brief one wasn't, and it's the collision that actually fires, because format requirements are what prompts most often state. Settled as: **Brief scores compliance with what was stated; Format scores fitness regardless.** They're proved distinct by a divergent case — ignoring *"one page"* to deliver two pages with proper working space fails Brief and scores 4 on Format.
  2. **`Polish` was the only one of four scales with no diagnostic column** — and by the 07-23 note it is the most contestable point in the rubric. `amateur → professional` imports an unstated comparison class, and the anchor correcting it sat in prose below the ladder where a scorer never meets it. Given the second column.
  3. **`Polish` and `Format` both claim visual hierarchy.** Candidate seam (agent, not ruled): the **thumbnail test** — Polish flaws show at a glance, Format flaws only appear in use.
  4. **`Format` as a name points at a ruled-out concept.** 07-24 killed scoring the file container; the word says container. A stronger reason to replace it than the taste objections already logged.
  5. **Rung 0 is unreachable on `Polish` for some artefact types** (an image generator has no raw-unformatted state) — **checked and harmless**, because comparability lives within a task and a task fixes the artefact type. The generalisation rule binds *criteria*, not individual rungs.
  6. **Within-task weighting is only half-parked.** 07-24 dissolved *cross-task* weighting; within a task the criteria still need relative weight, and Form is where it bites because Polish swings from decisive on a poster to noise on a lesson plan. **Resolved by quantification, not mechanism** — see below.

**OWNER CALL — an overall score is needed on the card, with the detail breaking it into buckets.** Ruled against an agent recommendation to publish a **profile with no total** (four numbers, reader does their own weighting — argued on the grounds that equal weighting is itself an unstated claim, and that 07-24 had already accepted losing a headline one layer up). Owner took the total.

**OWNER CALL — it aggregates by the mean.** *"Whatever it is it needs to be simple and intuitive. Average (mean) fits."* The simplicity constraint was stated as governing the instrument, not just the arithmetic — which is now carried onto the Substance non-compensatory question.

**Agent-proposed, consistent with the ruling but not separately ruled — two levels, so the buckets are the weighting.** Bucket = mean of its criteria; overall = mean of the three buckets. Not a flat mean across all criteria, which would hand Substance ~55% of the score if it red-pens to five items — majority control of the number to the bucket 07-23 ruled is *a guard, not a discriminator*. Side effect worth keeping: **bucket size no longer affects bucket weight**, so Substance can be red-penned on the merits.

**Finding 6 answered with a number, not a mechanism.** Under the two-level mean, Polish is **one sixth** of the overall: scoring it 1 instead of 3 moves the overall 0.33 on a 0–4 scale, ~8% of the range. Small enough to live with. `N/A` stays as the single lever, reserved for a criterion with *no face at all* on that artefact type. The two alternatives were argued and rejected — per-task owner-set weights (every weight is a number to defend, forever, and a weight can't be shown to a reader the way a rung can) and blanket N/A (blunt where the problem is graded).

**OWNER CALL — the overall bands, in teacher units.** `3.5–4 glowing reference · 3.0–3.5 use straight away · 2–3 do some work · 1–2 do a lot of work`. Owner's rationale: *"4s should be pretty difficult to get."* First stated as *"3 means directly usable, 2–3 means edits necessary"*, then sharpened. **The 0–1 band label (*start over*) is the agent's answer to the owner's open question and was not ruled.** Band widths are deliberately uneven — coarse at the bottom, fine at the top. The arithmetic delivers the difficulty for free: a mean punishes any single weak spot, so 3.5+ needs every bucket near-perfect.

**OWNER CALL — a cap isn't an exception to the maths; the band label enforces itself.** *"It just changes what the average result means."* This overturned an agent argument that a cap would make the headline un-derivable from the visible detail and therefore spend exactly what the mean had earned. It doesn't: if 3.0–3.5 means *use straight away*, an artefact with wrong content cannot sit there, because the band would be lying. This is the honest form of the 07-23 *"substance failures must bite hard"* ruling, which a plain mean cannot deliver (accuracy at one-fifth of one-third is ~6.7% of the overall). *Agent generalisation, not ruled:* it isn't accuracy-specific — **the headline can't promise what the details deny** — and `Purpose` at 1 under a mean of 3.2 breaks the same band.

**OWNER CALL — the tool card carries two ratings: an overall tool rating and an outputs rating.** The rubric produces the outputs rating only. Raised by the owner as a defect in the bands: *"these descriptions may not fit because we are talking only about exemplar rating outputs"* — correct, and the mismatch was that a tool card was asking for a unit 07-24's architecture doesn't produce. Resolved by **different warrants**: the outputs rating is **measured** (evidence = the artefact, checkable), the tool rating is **judged** (evidence = the review prose). This completes the 07-24 *"nothing survives as a tool score"* finding rather than overturning it — the tool rating never claims to be derived, and 07-23 already ruled the benchmark *is* the owner's take.

**OWNER CALL — the recommendation is binary, not graded.** The owner floated **A–E** and moved off it on the agent's objection: A–E is the Australian school achievement scale, so to K–12 teachers it reads as criterion-referenced measurement — it would look *more* derived than the number it's meant to contrast with, and it's the one form in the instrument with no descriptor. Landed as **recommend / not**, with likes as a third, separate signal *when built*.

**OWNER CALL — there is no general "not approved" classification.** *"Not approved is CEWA linked. There is no such classification outside of CEWA."* This retired the agent's own counter-argument (the Prohibited-bucket ruling under Design) and left **positive-only** badging — recommended, or no badge — standing unopposed. *Not separately ruled, but the only objection was removed by the owner.*

**OWNER CALL — third-party safeguarding statuses are the exception that may carry a negative.** *"Unless it's from ST4S or whatever the digital safeguarding group is."* A **citation, not a judgement**: the site reports an assessment body's finding rather than making a claim. Publishable because the **source is public** — the operative line is public-source vs internal-source, not positive vs negative, which is the same line 07-24 drew ruling evidence public. Lives in the **disclose** slot (07-24). Backlogged for mining, with the body's name and status vocabulary flagged **unverified** and coverage of general-purpose AI tools expected to be thin. **Not a rebuilt CEWA badge.**

**Agent-framed, NOT owner-ruled (do not inherit as settled):**
- **The 0–1 band label** *start over*, and the reading that unifies the ladder's register — **what the output is worth to you** (worth nothing → salvaging → editing → using → telling people about). The register genuinely does break at the top, and structurally so: the work ladder **runs out of headroom at 3.0**, since there is nothing above "no work required".
- **The three-warrant display grammar** — number / badge / count, so measured, judged and popular signals can't be blended.
- **The tally** for a tool scored on several tasks ("usable straight away on 4 of 6"), on the grounds that a count needs no commensurability between artefact types and survives small n where a mean is false precision. Moot near-term: the pilot has one task, so the honest card is one score with the task named on it.
- **The thumbnail test** (Polish vs Format), and the fix wordings for all six findings.
- **The 3.5 boundary is the noisiest line in the instrument** — at `n=1`, 3.48 and 3.52 are the same artefact with very different words. Probable answer: the review carries the verdict and the band follows it.
- **The overall bands speak in `Purpose`'s register**, so the headline and one of its own inputs will sometimes contradict each other on one card. Three ways out tabled, none ruled.
- **`Brief`'s missing rung 4 is now structural** — Function caps at 3.5, so the overall maxes at **3.83** and the card can never show a full score.

**Process note — no artefacts were opened this session.** The stress test ran on theory alone and the offer to score the three pilot worksheets was made and deliberately not taken up. Fifth consecutive session the sample-as-data-source risk was live; first one where it didn't fire.

## Strategy (2026-07-29 — the red-pen: Function and Form are RULED; scales measure excellence of their own descriptor)

Sixth session of the rubric arc, and the first that **converted architecture into an instrument**. Step (ii) of the 07-24 re-sequence was executed for two of three buckets. The rubric now lives at [`delivery-rubric.md`](delivery-rubric.md) — a new canonical file, because the instrument's facts were previously spread across five DECISIONS entries.

**Net: `Function` = `Brief` · `Purpose`. `Form` = `Polish` · `Format`. Five drafted Function sub-criteria became two; four drafted Form sub-criteria became two. Substance is untouched.**

**OWNER CALL — the Function criterion, authored whole.** *"Could I use this as is in the classroom with students?"* with a 0–4 scale in **edit distance**. The owner wrote both the question and the rungs; the agent's contribution was testing and re-spacing. This is the moment the rubric stopped being architecture — the scale is in **teacher units** (edits convert to an afternoon), which is what makes it legible and what meets the owner's own 07-24 descriptor bar.

**OWNER CALL — rung 0 is `unsalvageable`, not "not possible".** *"basically should read unsalvagable"*. Fixes a collapse — "not possible" and "can't use" were the same sentence twice. Salvageable-or-not is a real decision a teacher makes: edit it, or bin it and re-prompt. **Generalised by the agent into a standing rule and accepted in use:** every rung 0 must name a state *distinct in kind* from rung 1, or 0 goes dead. Applied again to Polish (*nothing attempted* vs *a bad attempt*) and Format (*wrong format* vs *badly done*).

**OWNER CALL — the criterion is counterfactual: *"if it were possible to edit, how much would you need to change"*.** Corrects an agent flag that the scale smuggled in editability (a re-openable file being cheaper to fix than a flat one). It does not: the measure is the **amount of change required**, never the **cost of making it**. This keeps the criterion comparable across tools and avoids scoring the format rather than the output — the trap the owner already named on 07-24 (*"scoring a tool for choosing PDF would just be scoring PDF"*).

**OWNER CALL — "can a student do the work on it" folds into the main criterion.** *"If the kids can't do work on it, the worksheet can't be used."* And the decisive second half: *"if you wanted a worksheet that was pure information/instruction that would be different too"* — room-to-work is the **worksheet face** of usability, not a criterion. This is the 07-24 manifestation rule firing for the second time, which upgrades it from a one-off to a reliable test: **the criteria stay fixed; the task decides which faces become visible.**

**OWNER CALL — Function is two criteria: `meets the brief` · `fulfils the purpose`.** Owner proposed the pair (as "meets criteria / fulfils the purpose"); "criteria" was renamed to "brief" on an agent flag that it collides with the rubric's own word for its items. Everything else in the drafted Function list has a home elsewhere or is dead — see the cut table in `delivery-rubric.md`.

**OWNER CALL — the scale measures excellence of its own descriptor; above-and-beyond is a different metric.** The most consequential ruling of the session. Every 0→4 runs floor→excellent *within the thing being measured*, and nothing reaches outside itself. **This resolves the ceiling fork in both buckets at once** and partially answers the THREADS question *what does a `4` mean* — a 4 is the excellent end of that descriptor, full stop.
  - **Immediate consequence, applied:** the 4s already written were above-and-beyond and came off. `Purpose` and `Polish` were re-spaced to restore five rungs (Purpose gained *a tweak or two* at 3 and moved *hand it out untouched* to 4). **`Brief` was not re-spaced and currently tops out at 3** — carried as an open item.
  - The above-and-beyond **mechanism** stays parked (07-26). *Agent argument, not ruled:* a floorless quality — one that can be present or absent but never *bad* — is badge-shaped rather than scale-shaped, which is an argument for the badge option specifically rather than for bonus points.

**OWNER CALL — Form is two criteria: `Polish` · `Format`.** Reached by teasing apart the owner's own observation: *"One of them is just visual style and appeal... the difference between slapping elements on a page that are incoherent, to a full branding kit."* The second half is the format's interaction with the person using it.
  - **`Polish`** = do the elements belong to one designed thing. Unity is the *observable mechanism*, appeal is what it produces at the top — which converts a taste judgement into an inspection, and taste is the most contestable point in the rubric (07-23).
  - **`Format`** = *"how does the format interact with the user"* (owner, correcting an agent over-narrowing to attention-direction alone). Covers how you move through it, what it demands of you (flipping, stapling, zooming, scrubbing), and whether its affordances match the use.
  - **The seam with Purpose** (agent, accepted in use): Purpose measures what you'd *change*; Format measures what it costs you to *operate*. A four-page handout can need no edits and still make you staple and flip.
  - **Why Form cannot fold into Function** (agent argument, owner asked for it directly — *"honestly asking and i kind of know the answer"*): Function's scale is usability, so everything that makes an artefact **worse to receive without making it unusable** would disappear. That is the whole territory Form exists to hold, and it is why aesthetic was kept as Form's ceiling on 07-23.

**OWNER CALL — scale rungs must sit on one ladder.** *"the middle 2 don't tie in to the amateur-professional scale."* Killed *plain* and *considered* as Polish rungs — they describe **choices**, not **level**, so rung 2 could apply to a rung 4 artefact. Settled: `no design attempted · amateur · competent · skilled · professional`.

**OWNER CALL — Format's scale must be positive, not a ladder of obstruction.** The agent's first draft ran *blocks you → you fight it → you work around it → stays out of your way → works for you*. Recast as fitness: `wrong format · crude · workable · well suited · tailored to how it's used`. **Both were kept** — the positive column publishes, the negative column is the diagnostic you score with, because it names what to look for. Same dual register as everywhere else.

**OWNER OBSERVATION — the naming convention is nouns with questions underneath.** *"i think we would eventually bring it back to the nouns anyway."* This is the site's existing ACME pattern (`A · Audience` over *Who am I teaching?*), so it is not a compromise between the agent's verb phrases and the owner's nouns — it is the house style. Set: **`Brief · Purpose · Polish · Format`**.

**Agent-framed, NOT owner-ruled (do not inherit as settled):**
- **The relational test for scale words.** Rungs that describe a *relationship* travel across modes; rungs that describe a *feature* are mode-bound. The owner's *"those words fit nicely across modes too"* is the observation; the rule extracted from it is the agent's. It has since predicted correctly three times, but it has not been ruled.
- **The enumerate-the-edits scoring procedure** — list what you'd change, rate the size of the list. Offered as the resolution of the whole-artefact-vs-part-by-part scope question (which the agent had raised as a fork and then withdrew, since component failures cannot wash out of an enumerated list). The owner did not rule it; it is recorded in the instrument as procedure.
- **`artefact` vs `content` is a scale, not a criterion** — the same two axes (organised, unified) run at both magnifications, which is why coherence kept appearing on both sides. Proposed to collapse three candidates to two; the owner moved on without ruling it, and the two-criterion outcome is consistent with it.
- **The pedagogy line on attention science.** The owner asked *"surely theres a science to creating attention in the right spaces for any given mode"* — correct, and it is one cross-mode framework (gestalt grouping, salience/hierarchy, the signalling and coherence principles) rather than one map per mode, which answers their own earlier mapping objection. **The agent's line, not ruled:** judge whether the artefact communicates *its own* structure, not whether that structure suits the learner — the first is communication, the second is a teaching decision and belongs in the brief. Without this line, cognitive-load theory re-imports pedagogy into a delivery-only score.
- **Naming rejections** — *student friendly* (fails the artefact-type test: lesson plans, parent emails and marking rubrics aren't student-facing; and it crosses the pedagogy line), *UX* and *user design* (jargon, and refusal #7 is "no jargon walls"), *Experience* (a superset word — Polish and Purpose are both part of your experience, so it reads as the parent of its siblings), *Fit* (collides with "fit for purpose", with Purpose sitting next to it). **`Format` is explicitly a working name** — *"keep format for now"*.

**Carried items, both already discharged before this session:**
- The **07-23 evidence-note amendment** (the "worksheets are ugly and unusable" prior came from Microsoft Copilot, not from weak prompting) was already banked in place on 2026-07-26 at the foot of the 07-23 entry. No action needed.
- The **possible fourth Copilot artefact** is already on BACKLOG. Owner clarified 2026-07-29 that **the only sample files are in `Desktop/Projects/`** — an agent search of `~/Downloads` was out of scope and its find is not an artefact.

**Process note — the sample-as-data-source failure mode recurred.** Owner: *"i need you to stop referencing the samples because it's not helpful in this theoretical discussion. Only reference them if it provides extra insight and furthers the concept."* This is the same correction as 07-24 (*"the example is a thinking prop, not a data source"*) and the second half of the session ran materially better without them. **The honest counter-weight:** in the first half the artefacts did real work the theory could not — three tools failing three *different* Form criteria is what established those criteria weren't collapsible. ## Strategy (2026-07-26 — the pilot review: visible/invisible, core function vs judgement call, and the first evidence the rubric discriminates)

Fifth session of the rubric arc and **the first with real artefacts in hand**. The owner's call was to stop trying to author the whole task suite and instead **land one review end-to-end**, using three outputs they already had. This produced the first falsifiable material the benchmark has generated — architecture cannot be wrong in a way anyone would notice; three scored artefacts can be.

**OWNER CALL — pilot one task end-to-end, ahead of the task list.** Task: the Y7 adding-fractions worksheet. Entrants: `ChatGPT - Sol - High`, `Anthropic - Opus 5 - High`, `Canva - CanvaAI`. Owner's rationale: *"one stable example to launch other work from."* **This does NOT discharge the task-list job** — the suite is still owed (BACKLOG). What it discharges is the blocker: sessions 3 and 4 both stalled for want of artefacts to red-pen sub-criteria against.

**OWNER CALL — Canva takes the same prompt input, so the trio is a controlled comparison.** *"Canva has an AI prompt input; so it is the same initial conditions."* Identical prompt text, three tools, single-shot. Resolves the agent-raised fork (same prompt text vs a tool-appropriate expert prompt) **for this trio only**. The general case — a tool with no prompt box at all — is unruled and does not need ruling until one appears.

**OWNER CALL — the visible/invisible model. This is the first real answer to "what is the ruler."**
  - **Visible** = how well did the tool execute what was explicitly written in the prompt.
  - **Invisible** = how well did it execute on *assumed or vague* information that is **still required to complete the task**.
  - *"Both should be the same in total for a given task but will have that different filter depending on the prompt."* The requirement set is fixed per task; the prompt does not change it, it only moves the line between told and assumed.
  - **Amends the unratified 07-24 agent refinement** *"the ruler is the brief you wrote"* — a brief can *reference* a standard it never restates (`Year 7` summons the whole curriculum expectation), and that standard is part of the ruler.

**OWNER CALL — preferences are still measured against the instruction.** *"some are preferences but the measurement would still be on how well was formatting executed against the instruction."* Kills an agent objection that stated preferences (e.g. "3 columns") break the fixed-total claim by appearing from nowhere. They don't: **the conserved thing is the set of axes, not the set of sentences** — layout is required for any worksheet, and "3 columns" merely particularises it. The preference-vs-requirement distinction is therefore **true but inert**, and folds. *(Possible second life in **weighting** — "is the maths right" plausibly outweighing "is it three columns". Not ruled.)*

**OWNER CALL — Substance discriminates when the brief is loose; this amends the 07-23 evidence note.** *"In this particular prompt, Substance will actually be a defining differentiator between tools compared to a prompt that has it all explicitly written."* Owner's own diagnosis of why 07-23 concluded otherwise: *"this was due to assumption that substance would be dictated, but i dont think we thought of what happens when its a loose description and not exact."* The 07-23 note (accuracy trends to table-stakes, so discrimination sits in Form/Function) was right **for a heavily-specified prompt** and wrong as a general rule.
  - **Confirmed by the evidence, with a refinement:** every sum in all three artefacts is arithmetically correct, so **accuracy was table-stakes exactly as 07-23 predicted**. Substance still discriminated — through **completeness and alignment** (Canva supplying 8 of 12 questions and omitting an entire requested category) and **craft** (ChatGPT dropping the conversion step in its hardest worked example). Both rulings describe different parts of Substance.

**OWNER CALL — core function vs judgement call decides what the invisible half can be scored on.** *"for anything that is core function, it is comparable. for anything where its judgement call, its an interesting thing to note."* Answers the agent-raised risk that "the brief didn't specify, so be more specific next time" would collapse the invisible half into the prompt's fault and make it unscoreable.

**OWNER CALL — unrequested additions cut both ways, and a correct answer key is a pro.** *"Any time the tool gives the user something they didn't know they wanted or needed is a win. The opposite can also be true though, anytime the tool gives you something you dont want thats bad."* On the specific case: *"Answers are always a pro because it validates the worksheet, ensuring it works."* This defeated the agent's "teacher-relative, therefore prose not score" objection — a key is not a convenience item, it is **evidence the artefact works** (the questions are solvable and the tool knew the answers), which holds for any teacher. *(Agent rider, unratified: this holds while the key is correct — a wrong key is worse than none, because it launders an error into something a teacher trusts.)*

**OWNER CALL — the score must have room to award better-than-prompted output.** *"Yes we are trying to score for how accurate to the prompt the tool is being; but there should be something in there that awards a better output than was prompted."* Consistent with 07-23's floor→ceiling design, where every bucket already runs *did what was asked → did it excellently*.

**OWNER CALL — ordered-set tiers are dead; the above-and-beyond MECHANISM is PARKED.** Bronze/silver/gold rejected — *"primarily because its an ordered set"*. Explicitly parked: *"Put the idea of how to differentiate above and beyond for another time."*
  - **Live:** the requirement that something must recognise better-than-prompted output.
  - **Reopened by the parking:** what a `4` means. The agent had framed it as "did everything asked, excellently", with excess living in a separate bonus — which only holds if a bonus mechanism exists. Until the mechanism is settled, **the top of the scale is undefined**.
  - Options explored and left on the table, none ruled: score-bound bonus points above the maximum; non-score-bound badges / honourable mentions; a capped rank plus an uncapped accumulator (the competitive-gaming "Platinum + stars" shape).

**OWNER OBSERVATION — the prompt should request answers, to test whether the tool can produce them.** *"its not that i need verification; i need to know that the tool can produce accurate answers."* The sheets prove these tools can **pose** Year 7 fraction questions; they barely prove any can **solve** one (three worked examples each, plus Canva's key). **Not applied to this pilot** — the three artefacts came from the current prompt, and single-shot means changing it invalidates the comparison. Banked as **v2 of the canonical worksheet prompt**. Owner also noted the absent curriculum outcomes as *"professional development learnings about how specific you need to be"*.

### Artefact findings (first three-way comparison; all PDFs read in full, not recalled)

- **Visible half — Canva fails six of ten stated instructions:** four pages against an explicit *"single sided"*; one column instead of three; 8 questions instead of 12 (plus four "invent your own"); the requested **unrelated-denominators category is never supplied as questions**; a single dotted line under an instruction reading *"show your full working out"*; and `1. Model: 1/4 + 2/4 = ?` followed by a blank line — **it printed the word and never demonstrated anything**. ChatGPT and Opus 5 pass essentially the whole checklist.
- **The invisible half is demonstrably scoreable.** One ambiguous instruction (*"the first question of each type should be a model question"*) produced a clean three-way spread: **Opus 5** badged it `WORKED EXAMPLE`, showed every step, explained the reasoning, and withheld the answer line; **ChatGPT** solved it but left it numbered like a live question with full blank working space (three of twelve slots dead), and dropped the conversion step in its hardest column having shown it in the easier one; **Canva** did not demonstrate at all. **This substantially answers the THREADS question "what catches correct-but-self-defeating"** — it is *unstated but core function*, and therefore scored. Which bucket it lands in is still open.
- **The 07-24 defect is confirmed and re-diagnosed.** The worked examples *are* the answers to Q1/5/9 — but the instruction, read literally, asked for exactly that. The tool complied; the brief was ambiguous. What remains a genuine tool failure is reserving full blank working space beneath an already-solved question. **Ambiguity in a brief is revealed by divergence across tools** — a useful diagnostic.

Explicit call: don't let alliteration cost the most valuable, least-obvious axis found that day. `Fidelity` also rejected as a bucket name — overloaded (used generically across all buckets); `Fitness` rejected as bleeding into Function.
  - **Every bucket runs floor → ceiling**: *did what was asked* → *did it excellently*. Substance: correct → considered (craft). Form: renders → beautiful (aesthetic). Function: works → works well. **Aesthetic is therefore not its own criterion — it's the top of the Form scale.** Accepted cost: the aesthetic ceiling is the **most contestable, most subjective point in the whole rubric** (a great-looking Year 2 sheet ≠ a great-looking Year 11 one), included knowingly because polish is real value.
  - **No double-counting.** If something is unusable *because* Form or Substance failed, it is billed **once, upstream, where the fault is** — Function is not a second bill. Function scores only the operational residue nothing else explains (runs, editable, prints, interactive). Prompted by the owner's observation that "if either content or presentation fail enough, it becomes unusable" — i.e. usability is largely **emergent** from the other two, not an independent peer.
  - **Why Function survives as its own bucket** despite that emergence: the benchmark scores **tools**, not just documents. For interactive/editable outputs (quiz apps, editable docs, slide exports) "does it actually run / can I adapt it" is independent of looks — a slick quiz with broken answer-checking, a beautiful doc delivered as an uneditable flat image.

The owner's prior experience ("outputs are just text on a page… ugly and actually unusable") did **not** reproduce, plausibly because the prompt was far more exact and the model more capable.

The model is now fully settled: structure, stages, inputs, depth-meaning, and public register all closed. This entry is the canonical statement; the earlier 2026-07-22 entry below records how the stage *names* were chosen (superseded only on the E wording and the register).

- **OWNER CALL — the model carries two registers by design (progressive disclosure, the site's standard pattern):**

    | **A** | Audience | Who am I teaching? |

    | **M** | Method | How will I teach it so it sticks? |

- **OWNER CALL — agent = teacher, object = learner, throughout — but the stages are not forced into identical grammar.** A late owner challenge ("should this all be framed around the learner instead of the teacher?") was tested against the set and revealed the stages don't sit on one side: the **inner two (What, Method) are teacher-active** (the teacher selects content and designs method — method especially is the teacher's lever, and learner-framing it wrongly implies students choose the pedagogy), while the **outer two (Audience, Evidence) are learner-described** (who they are, what they learned). Forcing everything learner-side makes What go passive and How go wrong; forcing everything teacher-side makes Evidence self-centred. Conclusion: **the teacher is the agent of the loop (keeps the improvement engine and the Catalyst axis attached to a subject — a full learner-frame would orphan "who is improving?" and turn a *teacher improvement* model into a generic learning-design model, cutting against the site's "teacher holds the judgement" spine); the questions point at the learner as their object.** Grammar varies by stage honestly, because teaching *is* a teacher acting on behalf of learners.

The progression across the session: first fixed from *teaching* to *learning* (you can teach well and they still not learn — evidence of learning is the honest measure, and it feeds next turn's Audience better than evidence of teaching); then chosen in its "how is it evident" form because it asks the teacher to *define what learning even looks like* (success criteria, indicators) rather than just collect after the fact — the richest, most experienced-teacher-provoking phrasing, which is exactly the depth bar. **Known accepted cost:** it starts with "How", as does the Method question, so two questions open on "How"; judged worth it — provocation over tidiness — since the full questions aren't truly ambiguous.

## Strategy (2026-07-22 — the improvement model's stages: Who · What · How · Evidence)

- **OWNER CALL — the four stages are named `Who` · `What` · `How` · `Evidence`:**
  - **Who** — who am I teaching. Deliberately plural by default (a class, not a learner) — differentiation is not a separate stage or a closing check bolted on, it's what "who" already means once the honest answer is a range, not a single profile.
  - **What** — what am I teaching (curriculum fidelity, learning intentions).
  - **How** — how will I teach it so it sticks (delivery — CLT, Rosenshine, explicit instruction).
  - **Evidence** — did it work, for whom, what will I do about it. Closes the loop: this stage's output becomes the next turn's `Who`, matching the continuity ruling below almost word for word ("what the evidence says becomes the starting point of the next turn") — which is why the name was chosen over the alternatives it beat.
- **Differentiation folded into `Who`, not given its own stage.** It nearly became a closing "who isn't with me" check, separate from the front-loaded "who am I teaching." Collapsed on the reasoning that a single class is always multiple learners, so a real answer to "who am I teaching" already implies the range. `Evidence` inherits this for free — "did it work, for whom" asks the differentiation question without a dedicated slot.

  - `Review` — collides with shipped vocabulary (tool *reviews* are core benchmark terminology).
  - `Feedback` — already has a specific, opposite-direction job here: the Ring-3 scope decision below defines feedback as the *outward* half of marking ("marking gathers the evidence, feedback acts on it" — feedback *to students*). This stage is the *inward* half (evidence about whether the teaching worked, feeding the teacher's own next turn), so `Feedback` risked misreading in the wrong direction. Also self-referential: the model's continuity is already a feedback loop structurally, so the word would be doing two jobs at two altitudes.
  - `Check` / `Reflect` — plausible, not wrong, but break the part-of-speech rhythm of `Who`/`What`/`How` (short interrogatives/nouns vs a verb).
  - `Evidence` — no vocabulary collision found (checked against content JSON, routes, component names), and it's the exact word the continuity ruling below already uses, so it introduces no new vocabulary.
- **OWNER CALL — `Catalyst` does not split into mechanisms vs domains; "domains" is retired as a term in this model.** That split was an AI framing from the 2026-07-21 session, carried forward and reused in this one without checking it had actually landed — see the owner's process note in the entry below ("there are things that are owner decisions vs your interpretation"). ## Strategy (2026-07-21, later — the improvement model's structure: stage · context · catalyst)

> Same caveat as the entry below: **the structure is committed, the contents are not.** The stages themselves remain a working sketch.

- **The model is continuous** (owner) — the final stage leads back into the first rather than terminating. What the evidence says becomes the starting point of the next turn. This also resolves the earlier "pre and post a learning event" requirement without a second set of questions: the post of one turn *is* the pre of the next.
- **Each stage carries two kinds of input, and they are inputs to the *improvement* process, not to the teaching process** (owner correction — an AI reading of them as inputs to the teaching question was wrong).
- **OWNER CALL — the three components are named `Stage` · `Context` · `Catalyst`:**
  - **Stage** — the step in the loop.
  - **Context** — situational input. Improves *this turn's* answer: better information, same you. (Prior knowledge, diagnostics, what was covered last year, known misconceptions, work samples, and data about your own practice — video, student voice, a colleague's eyes.)
  - **Catalyst** — developmental input. Improves *every future turn's* answer: better you, regardless of situation. (Coaching, mentoring, observation, moderation, reading research, developing subject or specialist knowledge.)
  - The distinction that keeps the two apart: **one changes this turn, the other changes every turn.** If the names stop carrying that, the columns blur.
  - Chosen over `fuel`, `spark`, `accelerant`, `lift`, `whetstone`, `impetus`, `traction`, `lever`, `kindling`, `nudge`, `provocation`. Deciding arguments: a catalyst causes change *without being consumed* (coaching and research aren't used up), it matches "context" in register, and it is the only shortlisted option that survives as a countable plural — stages will list several. Known imperfection: strictly a catalyst changes a reaction's *rate*, not its outcome, which undersells what coaching can do.
  - **Ruled out as labels because they collide with shipped vocabulary:** `prompt` (core benchmark vocabulary), `capability` (`capabilities.json`), `domain` (tools taxonomy, parent of work type), `practice` (an existing track id in `frontend/src/content/`).
- **Catalysts close the local-optimisation gap.** An AI objection held that a self-feeding loop only polishes a teacher's existing repertoire, with no route to a technique they have never met. Coaching, mentoring and observation are that route — new knowledge enters the loop through catalysts.
- **Catalysts, not geometry, carry the improvement claim.** A closed loop implies you return to where you started; a spiral implies rising. Since catalysts are what make the next turn higher, the model can stay drawn as a loop without the shape having to argue for improvement.
- **OWNER CALL — naming something in the model does not commit us to building content for it.** Behaviour management, mentoring and relationships can appear as catalysts or contexts while tool alignment and editorials for them stay deferred. Model completeness is cheap and honest; content is where the cost is. (This supersedes an AI "bloat" objection that conflated the two.)
